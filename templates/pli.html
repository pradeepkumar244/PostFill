How the calculator works

Bonus is estimated as sum assured ÷ 1,000 × bonus rate × policy term in years, and the maturity amount is the sum assured plus that bonus. For example, ₹20,00,000 over 25 years at a bonus rate of ₹52 gives 2,000 × 52 × 25 = ₹26,00,000 bonus and ₹46,00,000 at maturity. The bonus rate is declared by the department and can change from year to year, so it is not guaranteed. You can edit the bonus rate above, or clear it to hide the bonus lines.

The rate table gives the monthly premium for every ₹1,000 of sum assured. Your premium is that rate multiplied by your sum assured divided by 1,000. For example, a rate of 5.15 on a sum assured of ₹1,00,000 gives 5.15 × 100 = ₹515 a month.

The rate depends on two things. A younger entry age costs less, and a longer term spreads the cost over more years, so it lowers the monthly premium. Combinations with a term shorter than 5 years are not in the table, which is why some maturity ages are missing for older applicants.

Tips before you apply

  • Compare two or three maturity ages. A longer term gives a lower monthly premium but you pay for more years.
  • Choose a premium you can keep paying. Stopping early can reduce what you get back.
  • Keep your age proof ready, as the premium depends on your age at entry.
